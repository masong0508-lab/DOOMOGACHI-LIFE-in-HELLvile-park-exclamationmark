## 1. The show in one breath

**Series logline.** A silent newcomer and a pet that only says "beep." move into the happiest town anywhere, and slowly learn that HaHaland stays happy by pumping every sad and scary feeling under a Wall into the neighbours it refuses to talk about.

**Premise.** Sunny arrives on a pink bus with one suitcase and nowhere they belong. The town gives them a cottage with a pink door, a key baked into a muffin, and an egg that hatches into a tiny creature whose whole vocabulary is "beep." Everyone is lovely. Everyone giggles. Nobody talks about the other side of the Wall. Then the fountain giggles half a second too late.

Across three seasons the audience digs up the town's secret one clue at a time, exactly as the game's story does:

* **The engine of the town is plumbing.** Ten years ago HaHaland learned to swallow its glums and flush them through the Giggling Fountain, under the Wall, into Hellvile. The "demons" that possess Sunny's friends are that breath, each one named after the Hellvile worker whose lungs it passed through. (Revealed at the series midpoint, S2E7.)
* **The face under the twist is a pet.** The cloud that fed on all of it was Fluffy, a pet cloud that drifted away from a nine-year-old cherub one Mingle night and came home thirty years later, huge and purple and lonely, to a town too scared to recognise him. (Revealed in the series finale: "...Hi, Fluffy.")
* **The cure is not a bigger gun.** The Wall falls when 1000 critter-folk each say the one thing they were scared to say.

**What the show is about.** HaHaland's cure for everything is not saying it. Every episode, someone on this side says one thing out loud, and the town gets a little less happy and a lot more real.

**Tone.** The game's own description is the target: cute first, with horror and gore that are funny because the first hour is a sugary daydream. The pastel world goes wrong in the background, then in your house, then in the sky. Every scare is undercut or doubled by a joke, except the few that are chosen not to be.

* **Comparable shows (the only comps used in pitch material):** Gravity Falls, The Owl House, Amphibia, Over the Garden Wall, Hilda.
* **Game references (named as games, not as tone comps):** Tamagotchi, Tomodachi Life, Animal Crossing, Undertale, DOOM.
* **Retired from all pitch material:** Shaun of the Dead and Hazbin Hotel. They describe the game's adult edges, which the show does not carry (judge 1, graft G20).

## 2. Audience, rating and the boundaries that bind every episode

| Area | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | Core 10-16, safe for family co-viewing. Every episode must play for a 10-year-old and a parent in the same room. |
| **Rating** | US TV Parental Guidelines: **series TV-PG (D, V) maximum**; never cut "hotter" for any market. V is cartoon fantasy violence against demons and hell-stuff only. D is mild innuendo, and only ever from Grumbleguts (first heard in 113). Equivalents per territory are confirmed with each buyer's standards team. |
| **Gore** | Confetti-gibs only: confetti, hearts, plush stuffing, grape goo. The game's BRUTAL gore setting and Monster Mode are not in the show. In the show, "brutal" names a register (first person, metal, hellified palette, HUD), never a gore level. |
| **Weapons** | The Bubble Wand (the merchandise lead) and Rivet's **Pump Sprinkler**, a welded junk contraption of bellows, drainpipe and bike bell that fires sprinkle shells. Never aimed at a citizen, never a realistic gun silhouette, never merchandised. The game's realistic weapon frames do not appear. |
| **Kids and teens** | Pip, Wisp, Bolt, Tantrum, Pokefork, Wobbles, Miss Lulla's daycare kids, Homework and every TEEN-flagged citizen are fully out of romantic or suggestive material and out of any scene that carries it. No child is ever possessed on screen. Kid peril stays mild: the fog never touches a child. A kid's big moment is courage. |
| **Grumbleguts** | Innuendo and slapstick only, never explicit; adults only; turned down kindly every time; the joke is his vanity and appetite, never his target; Sunny is never his target. Show-safe model (uniform, no harness, no crop). The full rules and the approved gag list are in `GAG_BIBLE.md` §7. Excluded outright: the S186 Feast outcome, the adult-humour passes in `GRUMBLEGUTS_TONE.md`, the concept-sheet items listed in `GAG_BIBLE.md` §7.6. |
| **Vaping** | Cloud Nine Vapor is the villain's lure and is always unglamorous satire of predatory marketing ("First puff's free... That's the business model."). No sympathetic character is shown inhaling: the possession inhale is always a covered cut. No real-looking devices, no real brands. Standards fallback: Cloud Nine becomes a "cloud candy" lounge with no other script change. |
| **Possession and faith** | Possession is stillness, red eye sockets, a doubled "we" voice running 267 ms late, and ears twitching too fast. No body horror, no harm to the host. Father Fuzz's rite is a hymn, holy water and a name, shown with affection and never mocked. No pentagram imagery in Season 1 (the game's pentagram rug becomes Vex's crowned-cloud rug, §5.6). |
| **Flashes and inserts** | Inserts run 4 frames or longer, never 1 (subliminal-image codes such as Ofcom rule 2.11). White flashes last at most 3 frames at reduced luminance. Full-screen red flashes are not used; failure flashes are local. PEAT check on every cut, Harding pass before delivery. |
| **Who the joke is on** | The town's avoidance, a character's vanity, appetite or denial. Never a kid's fear, never Hellvile for being Hellvile, never a real person, never an accent (Steamy's lilt is cast as a performer's own or a defined fictional lilt). |

**Why the format changes at 108.** Before "The Bottle" the audience must not see Hellvile's people, or the twist "the demons are people" is spent (judge 2). After 107 the twist is the point: the audience has just been told "That was a PERSON.", so from 108 the show goes and meets them. This gives buyers a format refresh inside Season 1, and the finale of the series closes the split screen for good when the Wall falls.

### 3.2 The main title and its living variants

"Doomtastic Times" is boarded by song section (`PRODUCTION_ROADMAP.md` §4.6) and conformed to the master when it lands in `docs/show/theme/`. The opening is built as one layered project with per-arc toggles, so each variant costs one render.

| Variant | Airs | The one detail that changes |
|---|---|---|
| **v1** | 101-104 | The base opening. Pilot only: the end pose dissolves to GIGGLE NIGHT with MINGLE showing through the paint. |
| **v2** | 105-113 | After Arc 1 closes on the glow blinking back (104), the far purple glow in the Wall shot **blinks back** at the camera. |
| **v3** | S2E1-S2E7 | After 113, **the gate has a crack**. (The round silhouette that bounces once behind it has been drawn from the show-safe Grumbleguts sheet since v1.) |

| **v5** | Series finale | **The pan has no Wall.** The split end pose becomes one frame. |

The pre-chorus is the **mirrored knock**: both sides run at the Wall and knock on the same beat. The Hellvile side is drawn in silhouette only until each character's canon reveal, so the opening never spends a twist.

### 3.3 Eyecatches

Built on the whiplash-insert rule (`GAG_BIBLE.md` §4.16).

* **OUT card (5 s):** the episode's key prop on a CUTE card, with the story clock (day and hour) in a corner. Scored with a 2-bar cut of the theme's hook in major; the Hee-Hee bell on frame 0.
* **IN card (5 s):** frame 0 is a 4-frame hellify flash; then the same card with **exactly one persistent change**, scored with the hook turned minor and resolving on the ITS_FINE sting. The change is the "previously on" for viewers back from the break.
* **The Mascotty runner:** she is somewhere in every IN card of the series, one step closer to camera each episode (an episode parameter of the template). She takes the head off only at the canon beat in S3 (game Ep 4.6). On a 2x11 centre card she is seen from behind, through the loose brick.
* **Every card is a seamless 5 s loop**, for streaming chapter markers and social posts (§7).

### 3.4 Act cards, credits and post-credits

* **Act cards are a piece of paper from the episode.** The pilot's are the Mayor's festival clipboard. Every episode has its own document (listed per episode in §5.3), drawn in the CREAM window skin, with the Hee-Hee bell on frame 0. This is cheap, it is diegetic text that localises from a string table, and it gives every episode a signature look.
* **End credits** are crayon storybook stills of the episode (history is crayon in this show), over a music-box arrangement of the theme's hook. The Mingle tune stays unfinished until the series finale.
* **The post-credits button is the gag the story was not allowed to have.** The final sincere beat of an episode is never undercut; its button goes after the credits. One deliberate exception: 107's post-credits is a sincere coda, the hinge of the format change (§5.3).

### 3.5 The Wall grammar

* **The camera can cross the Wall. Nobody else can** (until canon says so).
* **Geography:** in every shot that shows the Wall face-on, Hillvile is screen-left and Hellvile screen-right, so a cut across the Wall always reads.
* **The brick wipe:** every cut between the sides is a brick wipe along the Wall line (`GAG_BIBLE.md` §8.2 has the timing).
* **The Split:** when something crosses (a name, a knock, a hand at the loose brick), the wipe stops halfway and holds as a split screen, at least 2 s. The left half often reuses the A-segment's footage, which is free.
* **The split screen closes for good** when the Wall falls in the series finale.
* **Season 1's Hellvile is shot tight:** interiors, back lanes, kitchens, the south face of the Wall. There is **no wide establishing shot of Hellvile until S3E1**, when Sunny crosses with a record scratch. This protects the game's Ch4 reveal and keeps the B-segments cheap.

## 4. The episode engine

### 4.1 Three engines in one show

| Engine | What it is | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|
| **The Rite (procedural)** | A neighbour of the week is possessed. Clue props point to a real person across the Wall. Father Fuzz runs a three-part rite with three candles as visible stakes. Someone on this side says the name. | 101, 107, 109, 111; then the backbone of S2 |
| **The Mirror (standalone)** | A side mission from the game becomes the A-story; its other side becomes the B-story. Same day, same feeling, opposite side of the Wall. | 108, 110, 112; in Shape A episodes the side missions are B and C plots |
| **The Serial (mystery)** | One clue per episode, planted small and paid off later, mapped to the game's canon. The audience can solve the show before the town does. | Every episode (seed ledger, §5.5) |

On top of all three runs the **home spine**: the dream house changes phase with the chapters (COSY, HAUNTED, then BARRICADED in S2), and every episode ends at home.

### 4.2 The Rite, as a repeatable structure

| Step | What happens | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **The tell** | A neighbour stops bobbing. Ears twitch too fast. They say "we". Their voice has a second, flat voice under it, 267 ms late. | Stillness in a world that never stops moving is the scare. |
| 2. **The rating** | Possessors from Hellvile rate things out of ten ("Six. {d}Needs death."). | A Hellvile habit (Hillvile grades with letters: COSY: B+). The comic tell that a demon is a person with opinions. |
| 3. **Three candles** | Fuzz sets three candles on the nearest rail: "Three parts. The rhythm, the water, the name. Three tries." | The audience can always see how much is left. |
| 4. **The tries** | Rhythm (the hymn against the possessor's minor-key version of it), water (poured, never thrown at a face), name. Each fail: kazoo notes stepping down, one candle out, FAIL xN. | Rule of three (`GAG_BIBLE.md` §4.11). |
| 5. **The clue board** | Four names offered, one decoy that fits half the clues, and one prop that splits them, found where nobody looks. | Fair play: the audience can pick the name before the town does. |
| 6. **Sunny picks** | Sunny never speaks (three lines in the whole series, the first in S2), so Sunny finds the prop and picks the card. | Sunny is the detective, never the voice. |
| 7. **Someone says it** | A different Hillvile citizen breaks the taboo each time (§4.3). The name is never a joke; it gets an R13 hold before and after. | It can be a whisper. "Say it" does not mean shout it. |
| 8. **Home** | The breath leaves as a small tired puff and goes back over the Wall. The host's first line is about the person who was in there. | After 107 every rite carries "That was a PERSON." |
| 9. **The other side** | In a 2x11, the B-segment ends on the named person hearing their name (the Split). In a Shape A or B episode, a far-off reaction from beyond the Wall stands in ("...Seven."). | The canon line made visible: shouting a true name is "the first time in ten years anyone on this side has said that worker's name." |

**The Season 1 rites.** Only one soul is lost all season, and it is canon's scripted loss (Sprinklepop, 107). The show follows the "saved" branch of every authored rite (Steamy in 107; Hattie, Grampy and Lady Smog later).

### 4.5 The "Later" rule

Every episode plants exactly one **Later**: a prop, a line or a sighting that pays off in a later episode, in the same spirit as the game's side-mission callbacks (`story_final.md` §7 rule 10). The seed ledger (§5.5) is the writers' room's map of them.

### 4.6 How a writer builds an episode (checklist)

1. **Pick the engine:** Rite, Mirror, or an arc episode from the game's main path.
2. **Pick the game source:** the main-path episode and the side missions it adapts (Appendix A). Quote canon lines word for word; flag every change in the canon register (§5.6).
3. **Pick the paper:** the act-card document.
4. **Plant one Later** and pay one earlier seed.
5. **Check the house:** what phase it is in, and what the tag shows.
6. **Check the Wall:** what the episode may show of Hellvile (the Lock List, §4.7).
7. **Check standards:** §2 and `GAG_BIBLE.md` §11.
8. **Time it:** density and silence budgets from `GAG_BIBLE.md` §5; three protected gag-free holds in an event episode, at least one in every other.

## 5. Season 1: "Something in the Vapor"

### 5.1 Season shape

* **Arc 1, "Welcome to HaHaland" (101-104).** Make the audience love this town and this house before anything goes wrong. One event pilot, then three quiet episodes taken almost unchanged from Pitch A's material (graft G14), ending on the glow blinking back.
* **Arc 2, "Something in the Vapor" (105-113).** The wrongness moves from the background into people, then into Sunny's house. Slow scares and louder jokes, because everyone overcompensates.
  * **The turn: 107 "The Bottle".** "That was a PERSON."
  * **The refresh: 108-112.** The 2x11 format, alternating Mirror and Name episodes, while the vapor spreads and the little bed moves closer.
  * **The finale: 113 "The Cellar".** The gate cracks.

**Story calendar.** The pilot is Midsummer, Day 1, a Wednesday. Season 1 runs from Day 1 to Day 18. The game compresses Chapter 2 into days 5-9; the show stretches it to make room for the 2x11 episodes. Tallyworn's count (3,652 on the night of the rite) and the DAYS SINCE LAST FIRE sign follow the show calendar, and the bible re-derives the game's later numbers from it (for example Tallyworn's "3,661" at the gate in S3).

## 7. How the show and the game promote each other

### 7.1 The principle

The game is the show's playable bible; the show is the game's front door. They share one canon, one palette, one cast of 1000 and one score. Neither ever turns the other into an advertisement inside the work itself.

### 7.2 Guardrails (these come first)

| Guardrail | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Rating fit** | Get the game rated (ESRB, PEGI, or IARC for digital) before any cross-promotion. The current build carries the BRUTAL gore setting, Monster Mode, realistic firearm frames and the Grumbleguts adult-humour passes, so it will very likely rate above the show's 10-16 audience. Rating boards rate everything on the disc or ROM, including content behind a toggle. **The show never points its audience at a game rated above them.** |
| **The bridge** | Two show-safe products, each rated in its own right: **(a) a "HaHaland Edition"** build of Story Mode with CUTE gore only, the game's dedicated cutesy weapons only, no Monster Mode, Grumbleguts per `story_final.md` §3 hard rules with the S186 Feast outcome and the tone passes removed (target E10+ / PEGI 7, to be confirmed by the rating); **(b) the Doomogachi on its own**, the game's existing "Tamagotchi-only" mode as a free companion (target E / PEGI 3). The full game is marketed only to its rated audience, through age-gated channels. |
| **Children's advertising** | Follow the strictest common rules across territories: no game advertising inside episodes or placed adjacent to them; no show characters in ads next to the show (US host-selling policy for children's programming); no product placement of the game in episodes (Ofcom bans product placement in children's programmes); no calls to action, QR codes or "download now" in any episode. The production delivers clean breaks; commercial-time limits are the broadcaster's. Counsel confirms per territory. |
| **Online** | The YouTube pilot is designated per counsel's COPPA determination. No data collection from under-13s in any show-linked promotion. Social posts that point to the game run only on accounts and surfaces rated for the game's audience. |
| **Merchandise** | The Bubble Wand leads; the pet with a "beep." sound chip, Mr. Flops, the Population sign and the Board of Decency sticker sheet follow. The Pump Sprinkler is never merchandised. Grumbleguts merchandise, if any, is an adult line and never carries a flirt line. |
| **Trademarks** | Counsel's opinion on DOOMOGACHI, "Doomtastic Times", "Rip and tear... gently" and the HUD layout lands before anything public (`PRODUCTION_ROADMAP.md` §9 R3). No console logos in show or promotional material. |

### 7.3 Show to game

1. **The show is the TV-PG introduction to the world.** Viewers who are old enough find the full game; younger viewers find the HaHaland Edition and the Doomogachi.
2. **Rewatch clues are real in the game.** The fountain really is 267 ms late (S142 plots a histogram of it); every wrongness the show plants (W1-W16) is a thing a player can stand next to. "Did you notice the fountain?" works on both sides.
3. **Show nods in the game.** The show's inventions arrive as data-only updates or NG+ "HaHaland Remembers" extras, flagged as show nods in the canon register: Bonejangles' lantern-ribs look, the GIGGLE/MINGLE banner as decor, Clinker's forty-first chalk poem ending "...and we dream in pink." on the gate, a Pump Sprinkler weapon skin, and Drudge's beach postcard in their flat.
4. **"Doomtastic Times" ships into the game.** The game's Music Studio already supports song templates and a Concert Hall choir of 25 citizens (`docs/STUDIO.md`): the theme's hook becomes a Studio template, a Jukebox track and the title screen's attract loop. A fan remix campaign runs from the Studio, on surfaces rated for the game.
5. **"Hillvile Telly", a housing catalogue item.** A little TV for the dream house that plays the season's eyecatch loops at the game's own resolution (same palette, same cards), tagged "gift from Dippinn".

### 7.4 Game to show

1. **The roster is the casting bench.** Every one-beat name pill in the show is a real roster citizen with a model sheet already rendered (`core/tools/portrait_sheet.c`). Players spot their favourite resident in a crowd shot; the census crowd generator makes those crowds.
2. **Side missions are the show's standalones.** The game's 109 side missions are a ready slate of A-plots and B-segments, each already built on the trailer's joke anatomy and each with a "Later" button. In the game, adapted missions can carry an opt-in "seen on TV" mark in the quest log, for the game's rated audience only.
3. **The Midsummer calendar event.** The game runs on a real-time clock and already has a Mingle calendar event post-game. On the real Midsummer it can fire in the game on the same week the show's annual "Mingle Night" airs.
4. **The Library.** The game's Jukebox and Art Gallery wings unlock the show's main title, the eyecatch loops and pilot key art after the credits.
5. **What the game does not give the show:** player-made citizens from the Creator. User-generated characters raise rights and children's-privacy problems, so the show casts only from the authored roster.

| **Midsummer** (the week of June 19-24) | Pilot premiere (Option 1: Midsummer 2028); the annual "Mingle Night"; the main title released as a teaser a year earlier (Midsummer 2027) | The Mingle calendar event; a free show-nods update |

### 7.6 A shared pipeline

* **One palette** (`core/src/palette.c`) is the show's colour script and the game's art; the show's palette lint is the game's palette.
* **One score.** The game's leitmotifs (HOOK and RIFF as the same tune in major and minor, MINGLE in F, the Hee-Hee bell, the beep) are the show's underscore, and the theme's stems come back into the game.
* **One canon register.** Show decisions that the game wants (Sunny's pronouns as the default avatar label, show nods) go through the same DECISIONS file; the game never has to adopt a show change, and the show never contradicts `story_final.md` without a flag.

### 7.7 What we measure

* Pilot completion rate and the drop-off curve across 102 (does the quiet episode hold?).
* Shares per gag clip (the ratings runner, the hatch in the stampede, the Split at the brick).
* Theme streams and Studio remixes.
* HaHaland Edition and Doomogachi downloads from age-appropriate channels; full-game wishlists from age-gated ones.
* Buyer-side: requests for the bible and for the 102-104 outlines after the pilot screening.
