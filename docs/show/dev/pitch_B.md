**DOOMOGACHI LIFE in HELLvile Park!** Pilot, Episode 101

An ensemble sitcom pilot told in two 11-minute halves. The first half is set in HaHaland, the second in Hellvile, and the two collide at the Wall in the tag. Rated TV-PG (D), drawn in 16:9 pixel animation, with "Doomtastic Times" as the main title.

---

## 1. Title

* **Series:** DOOMOGACHI LIFE in HELLvile Park!
* **Pilot:** "Two Sides" (101)
  * **101A "Population: 501"** is the HaHaland side.
  * **101B "Open Late"** is the Hellvile side.
  * **Tag "The Loose Brick"** plays on both sides at once.

## 2. Logline

On the same Thursday, a silent newcomer bunny moves into relentlessly cheerful HaHaland and hatches a pet that only says "beep.", while on the forbidden side of the Wall a charcoal-baking imp granny tries to get her weekly cookies past a starving, shameless gatekeeper; at midnight, both halves of a park that never talks to itself reach for the same loose brick.

## 3. Format

**Runtime.** 22:00 for a 30-minute broadcast slot, built as two 11-minute segments. The streaming cut runs about 23:15: it adds a 5 s Dippinn Productions logo at the head and swaps the 20 s credit squeeze for the 1:30 TV-size theme. Each segment also stands alone for linear blocks that split episodes.

**The format is the premise.** Segment A runs from morning to midnight on the Hillvile side. Segment B replays the same Thursday, morning to midnight, on the Hellvile side. Four moments are seen from both sides:

1. The "1" falls off the sign in Hillvile and lands on the gatekeeper's lunch in Hellvile.
2. At 11:11, the moment the town freezes and swallows Sunny's question, a pipe on the other side heaves a purple sigh.
3. The midnight thumps are a scare in Hillvile. In Hellvile they are a lonely brother.
4. The sooty hand at the brick is a jump-scare in Hillvile and a squeal of joy in Hellvile.

**The Wall wipe.** Every cut between the sides is a brick wipe along the Wall line. The camera can cross the Wall; nobody else can. In the tag the wipe stops halfway and becomes a split screen. When the Wall falls in the series finale, the split screen closes for good.

**Run-down (broadcast).** Times are at 30 fps, so the comic-timing ladder (R2-R26) carries over frame for frame.

## 4. Target audience and rating

* **Rating.** The pilot is **TV-PG (D)**. The series ceiling is **TV-PG (D, V)**, where V means cartoon fantasy violence against demons only, and only from the first exorcism episodes on. Targets in other markets are UK PG (pre-watershed safe), Canada PG and Australia PG.
* **Audience.** The core is ages 10 to 16, plus adult animation fans (Gravity Falls, Over the Garden Wall, Hilda, The Owl House, Shaun of the Dead). Families can watch it together. Kids under 10 can watch with a parent: the scares come in the same beat as their joke.
* **What earns the D.** Grumbleguts' mild innuendo: "Come up and see my gate sometime. This is my gate." and "Nice buns." "They're MUFFINS." There is no swearing.

**Binding boundaries for the series:**

* **Gore** is confetti-gibs only: confetti, hearts, plush stuffing and grape goo. The game's BRUTAL setting and Monster Mode never appear. No cute character is ever shown hurt.
* **Kids and teens stay out of romance and innuendo entirely,** including any scene that carries it. That covers Pip, Wisp, Bolt, Miss Lulla's daycare kids and the teens. With kids, Grumbleguts is only ever the grumpy uncle. Flashbacks of kid Giggs and kid Nox are friendship only.
* **Grumbleguts** keeps his butt, belly and feast humour to innuendo and slapstick. He flirts only with adults, everyone turns him down kindly, and the joke is always his vanity and appetite, never the person he flirts with. His costume is softened to a gatekeeper's uniform: no harness, no crop, no unbuckling. Cut entirely: the post-game Feast outcome scene, every swearing or "adult humour" pass, the BRAAP gag, the hose close-up and the "...that's hot" crowd line.
* **Vaping.** Cloud Nine Vapor is the villain's lure, always unglamorous, with no real-looking devices or brands. In the pilot it is only a COMING SOON sign.
* **Horror ceiling.** Held stillness, red eyes in fog, inserts of 3-4 frames, and silence.

## 5. The pilot's story

**A-plot, HaHaland: "Population: 501."** Sunny, a silent young bunnykin with one suitcase, steps off the pink bus into a town so cheerful that the new "1" on the welcome sign falls off the moment it is added. Mayor Giggs gives her the grand tour. He points at the bakery, the fountain, the flowers and the lovely, lovely sky: everything except the enormous Wall through the middle of the park. Then he says, deadpan, "We don't talk about the other side." Sunny can't talk, so she points. Five hundred citizens freeze mid-bob and turn to stare at her, and the music only comes back with the Mayor's "Hee. Hee."

Nana Puddin gives Sunny her house key, baked into a muffin, and something else: an egg in a tea towel. She holds on to it one beat too long before letting go. In the Pink Door Cottage (rated COSY: B+), Sunny sits on her unopened suitcase and watches the egg wobble. It hatches on the downbeat. Two glossy eyes. A long silence. "beep." A caption appears: "...it's fine."

Sunny and her new pet help Pip, a teary little bunnykin, find their plush Mr. Flops. He is lying face down in the surf, staring at the Wall. Next to him floats a paper boat with a crayon house inside and "ARE YOU THERE" written backwards. Pip keeps it "safe, like Mr. Flops." That evening's housewarming introduces the rest of the town one gift at a time. The Mayor's toast slips ("To fluffy— ...cupcakes."), and on his walk home he leaves a cupcake on a child-sized grave: FLUFFY THE CLOUD. DRIFTED. Before bed, Nana gives Sunny her first real errand. She is to put a muffin in "the brick that wiggles" at the Wall after dark, "and don't wait." At midnight Sunny stands at the Wall with the muffin. From the other side comes a thump, three thumps, then one.

**B-plot, Hellvile: "Open Late."** This is the same Thursday from the other side. Hellvile is grim and cosy at once: bone lamps, soup steam, black lace on the washing lines, and kids playing hopscotch on lava in oven mitts. Charlotte is a round little imp granny whose cookies are 90% charcoal and full of love. She has not missed a Thursday at the loose brick in ten years. Today there is a new sign at the Wall: ALL PARCELS SUBJECT TO TOLL (SNACKS). It is signed GATEKEEPER GRUMBLEGUTS.

He is a red, pot-bellied, shamelessly vain imp devil who has sat on the sealed gate for ten years. His uniform has given up trying to button. He shares his voice with GURGLE, his belly, which is always hungry and always honest. He is waiting for a feast that someone promised him "when the gate opens." He flirts with Charlotte, who kindly turns him down. Then he eats her whole tin as the toll ("Six. Needs salt." "It's a texture."). Then he sits down, rear first, directly on the loose brick.

Charlotte goes looking for help, and her search introduces Hellvile. Tallyworn, a gate labourer, chalks another day onto a wall of marks and is cut off mid-count: "Three thousand, six hundred and—". Clinker writes chalk poems on the pumps. Signhere, the contract devil, has a clause for everything. Three plans fail, each bigger than the last: the night shift's work song, Signhere's page 412, and the biggest cookie in Hellvile, which arrives by forklift. In between, Grumbleguts shoos Rivet's little sibling Bolt away from the Wall: "Go home. Eat something. ...Not THAT." Out of plans, Charlotte does what nobody on either side ever does. She sits down next to him with her last cookie and asks out loud what he is waiting for. GURGLE answers first, and Grumbleguts translates: "...The belly says yes. The belly outranks me." He gets up. "Five minutes. Then it's toll." The sign on his side of the gate has said OPEN LATE for ten years. Tonight, for five minutes, it is true.

**C-plots.** In HaHaland, the Mayor spends the whole day rehearsing his welcome speech. It is one sentence long: "Welcome to HaHaland, where... hee. hee." In Hellvile, Thumpkin is a lanky "killer bunny" who thumps the ground every night until the neighbours move. Signhere serves him a 900-page noise complaint. Thumpkin won't say why he thumps, and at midnight he does it anyway: three, then one. The thumps that end Segment A as a scare are, from this side, somebody waiting for someone.

**Tag, both sides split by the Wall.** Sunny pushes the muffin into the brick and waits anyway. A sooty hand snatches it. The same three frames play in both halves of the screen: a jump-scare on the Hillvile side and a squeal of joy on the Hellvile side. A bag of charcoal cookies comes back through. Grumbleguts' eye fills the hole: "Evening, sugar. Nice buns." Nana told Sunny not to wait, and she waited too: "They're MUFFINS." "I'll allow it." GURGLE rumbles so hard that every lamp on Hillvile Lane flickers. NANA: "...Weather." Then the comedy stops. Nana lays her palm on the brick. "Every Thursday. She never missed one." [R13] "...Neither did I." On the other side, Charlotte's palm rests on the same brick. Far off down Hillvile Lane, something thumps back: three, then one. Thumpkin's ears lift. No button.

## 6. The cold open in detail: "The Egg Crossing" (1:10)

It is the series' visual thesis: two facing pages of one picture. There is paper grain and no character portraits. Captions are typed in a child's handwriting. The score is the Mingle song as a slow music box, in F, the key the series finale will lift into.

## 7. Act breaks (cliffhangers)

1. **A1 out (06:20), "...Mostly."** The egg's wobble speeds up from a 6 f period to 3 f, and a crack zips across it. SMASH to EC1 on the crack sound. The question going into the break is what hatches from an egg that came with a "Mostly."
2. **A2 out, the centre break (11:00).** Midnight at the Wall. Sunny holds the muffin, and the pet is in her hood. *Thump. Thump. Thump.* [R21] *...Thump.* Sunny freezes, the pet turns to face the Wall, and the purple glow pulses once. Cut. This is the pilot's real scare, and Segment B answers it.
3. **B1 out (15:40), the sit.** Grumbleguts plants himself rear first on the loose brick to a record scratch: "Nothing passes this Wall tonight. Not a crumb." The decency sticker slams in. Charlotte's empty tin rocks to a stop. A clock reads four hours to midnight. For the first time in ten years, a Thursday may go missing.
4. **B2 out (20:40).** There is no break here. The thumps hand straight into the tag, and the audience realises Segment A's cliffhanger was this moment all along.

## 10. The five best gags

1. **"beep."** The egg hatches on the musical downbeat and two glossy eyes pop open. The pet hops to the lamp and sits in the light. HOLD 21, music mutes for 29 f, then the smallest speech bubble in the show: "beep." R8 later a deadpan caption just *appears*: "...it's fine." This is the show's signature, its merch line and its "D'oh."
2. **The freeze.** Sunny silently points at the Wall. Five hundred citizens stop mid-bob on a tape-stop and turn to look at her in a ripple staggered 0-3 frames. HOLD 21. The music comes back on the downbeat with "Hee. Hee." On rewatch, the town clock says 11:11, and at 11:11 in Segment B a Hellvile pipe heaves a purple sigh.
3. **The "1".** The sign updates to 501, and R8 later the "1" falls off (*tink*). In Segment B it sails over the Wall onto the gatekeeper's bagels, and he pockets it "for later". After the credits he eats it: "...Needs salt." One gag, split across three acts and both sides of the Wall.
4. **"I only had a light snack—"** Grumbleguts' entrance, seen from behind, mid-meal on a mountain of Brimstone Bagels. R5 later a forklift reverse-beeps in with the next course. He stands and sucks his gut in [HOLD 13]; it springs out, and a waistcoat button pings off the forklift driver's hat. GURGLE: gurgle. "He says hello." A "Keeper of the Sealed Gate. Also of a very large seat." card pops in.
5. **The sooty hand.** The same three frames play as a horror sting on one half of the screen and a squeal of joy on the other. Straight after: "Evening, sugar. Nice buns." "They're MUFFINS." [R8] "I'll allow it." Then GURGLE flickers every lamp in Hillvile, and Nana says "...Weather."

*Bench:* the muffin rule of three (raisin, raisin, "that's a button", and the fourth bite crunches on an x-ray of a key with tiny horns); COSY: B+ and the crate sliding in shame; Thumpkin "signing" a 900-page complaint by thumping on the line; "Page 413 agrees with him."

## 11. How the pilot seeds the season arc

**The plumbing secret** (paid off at the series midpoint, "Under the Fountain")

* The fountain giggles R8, 267 ms, late during the Mayor's refrain.
* **The 11:11 sync.** Hillvile's town-wide swallow and Hellvile's purple sigh happen at the same minute. Viewers who rewatch after the midpoint will realise they saw the plumbing working in the pilot.
* **The split theme line.** The Mayor's "We don't talk about the other side." is answered by Clinker's "We don't ask where it comes from." At the midpoint the Mayor finishes the sentence: "...I never asked where it went."
* **Anvil's mark.** An anvil stamp sits on the pipe flange in the pump yard, and the same stamp is on the spoon at Anvil's empty place setting. Later Rivet finds her mum's mark under the fountain, and later still comes "It's a REALLY good spoon."
* **Tallyworn's count.** Possessed Steamy will taunt "We count. We count every day." and "Three thousand, six hundred and..." The first demon's true name, TALLYWORN, then belongs to someone the audience already knows. That gives "The Bottle" an extra twist.

**Fluffy and Vapula** (paid off in the finale, "...Hi, Fluffy.")

* The cloud on a string slips away across the book's gutter on Mingle night.
* FLUFFY THE CLOUD. DRIFTED. with a fresh cupcake on the stone, and the wings that stop flapping.
* "To fluffy— ...cupcakes.", with Nana's R8 look.
* Grumbleguts' feast promised "when the gate opens", and the 4-frame crowned cloud crossing the moon, in both the pilot and the main title.
* The purple glow pulsing R21 at the Wall, and the 1-frame LCD pentagram at the hatch.

**The Wall** (paid off when the Wall falls and the split screen closes)

* **The gate.** On the Hillvile side it reads KEEP OUT. FOREVER. under seven padlocks; on the Hellvile side it says OPEN LATE. At the season 1 finale Vapula leans on Grumbleguts and he belly-flops it open. It hurts more because we have liked him since the pilot.
* The loose brick and its Thursdays lead to the two-sided recipe card, "& Charlotte" on the bakery sign, and the Unity Loaf.
* The thumps and their answer lead to Mascotty and Thumpkin: "Taller." "...No."
* The scratched-out 1000, and the "1" the gatekeeper ate, lead to "Population: 1,001."
* "ARE YOU THERE" in backwards crayon leads to Wisp: "That's MY house. You KEPT it?"
* Cloud Nine's COMING SOON sign leads to Vex's grand opening.

**What the audience may know, and what stays locked.** This is the TWO SIDES rule.

| The audience may know early | Locked until its canon reveal |
|---|---|
| Hellvile is full of people; who sends the cookies; who thumps; that something comes through a pipe. | What is in the pipe and who ordered it; that the demons are breath; that Fluffy is Vapula; what happened on Mingle night and why the Wall went up; who Mascotty is; why the paper boats' sender can't come in person (Wisp). |

Segment B never goes inside the Pump room or the Underbelly. Vapula is only a silhouette until the season 1 finale, Wisp is never on screen in season 1, and Lady Smog appears only in her insert.

**Season map.** Three seasons of 20 half-hours each (40 segments per season):

* **Season 1, "Welcome to HaHaland" (Ch1-2).** E6: the candle, and the glow blinks back. E10: "The Bottle", the mid-season turn. E20: "The Gate Cracks", Grumbleguts' betrayal, and "THE DEMONS OF HELL ARE HERE."
* **Season 2, "Hell Invades" (Ch3).** E10: "Under the Fountain", the plumbing reveal and the **series midpoint**. E20: the dark night, when the pet runs over the Wall.
* **Season 3, "Hellvile Park" (Ch4).** The finale is "...Hi, Fluffy.", and the Wall falls.
* **Season 4, "Park Life" (optional).** Population: 1,000, with no split screen.

Throughout, the Hillvile segments carry the main story and the Hellvile segments draw on side missions, gatekeeper chaos and "Later" callbacks.

## 12. Why a network or streamer would buy it

1. **A hook in one breath, and a format that is the premise.** Two sides, one Wall, and a split screen that closes in the finale. It is a finite three-season arc, which streamers like, with an optional continuation after the Wall.
2. **Proven company in a fresh shell.** The mystery comedy and freeze-frame culture of Gravity Falls, the cosy-uncanny of Over the Garden Wall and Hilda, Undertale's "the monsters are people", and the cosy-sim audience of Tomodachi Life and Animal Crossing. One character brings Hazbin-style energy inside a TV-PG wrapper, which draws older fans in without a TV-MA.
3. **A flexible 2x11 format.** Linear blocks can schedule either half alone. The serialized mystery rewards bingeing. "The same moment from both sides" cuts naturally into paired clips for social.
4. **The IP already exists and plays.** A real GBA ROM and PS1 disc, a 78-second trailer, 1000 roster citizens with model sheets ("collect all 1000" blind boxes), a theme song built as an anime-style opening, and an in-game music studio for fan remixes. Show and game promote each other.
5. **Economical and distinctive.** It is pixel-native, reuses the game's textures, skies and model sheets, and gets a timed animatic on day one from the existing trailer toolkit. The cost per minute is low, and it looks like nothing else on the schedule.
6. **Substance parents can stand behind.** A town that stays happy by pumping its feelings at its neighbours, cured by saying it out loud. It deals with emotional honesty, prejudice and borders through plush comedy, without preaching.
7. **A theory engine.** One-frame inserts, a giggle that is 267 ms late, backwards letters, the 11:11 sync, and a hidden cameo in every eyecatch.

## 13. Risks, and how each is handled

1. **Grumbleguts drifting past the rating.** *Mitigation:* the show bible's rules apply (innuendo only, adults only, kind rejections, never mean). The uniform redesign replaces the harness and crop, the concept-sheet cuts listed in §4 are made, and standards review happens at script and at animatic. The decency sticker only ever covers trousers, and it is the first gag cut if standards object.
2. **Showing Hellvile early wears down the game's twists.** *Mitigation:* the locked list in §11. Every pilot plumbing clue is a rewatch clue, not information a first-time viewer can act on. The trade is deliberate: the audience gets the other half, so every exorcism lands on someone they know.
3. **Departures from canon.** Grumbleguts debuts in the pilot instead of Ep 2.7, and several of his later gags ("light snack", "belly outranks me", "Nice buns") move forward. *Mitigation:* Hillvile sees only his eye until the season 1 finale, and his heart line ("Nobody came up to see it.") and his redemption stay where they are.
4. **A silent lead.** *Mitigation:* Sunny is a physical-comedy lead (she points, freezes and waits anyway), the pet is her voice, and the ensemble does the talking. Her three canon lines become series events: the pet's name at the closed gate, the answer to Wisp, and the Say It finale. Audio description is a mandatory deliverable.
5. **Too much in 2x11.** The pilot introduces about 20 named characters. *Mitigation:* four leads per side. Everyone else gets one beat and a name-pill lower third. Gag density follows the comic-timing bible (one gag per 1.5-3 s in set pieces, one punchline per 3-6 lines, heart beats held 3-5 s with no gag), not the trailer's one every 1.7 s.
6. **Sensitive themes for ages 10-16:** vaping, exorcism, "Hell" and pentagrams. *Mitigation:* vaping stays off screen in the pilot and is always villainous after that. If a broadcaster requires it, Cloud Nine becomes a "cloud candy" lounge. Faith is portrayed warmly and never mocked (the plush priest of St. Cuddles, Nox's backwards rite). Pentagrams appear only in 1-4-frame LCD inserts.
7. **Clearance.** "DOOM", the Tamagotchi-adjacent title, and third-party design references. *Mitigation:* clear the title early, with "HELLvile Park!" as the fallback. Pitch art is original only; `docs/design/story/refs/*.webp` is never shown.
8. **The pipeline.** The game's scenes use hard-coded 240x160 layouts. *Mitigation:* the production plan below. The pilot animatic comes first, then the scene re-layout.
9. **Localization.** The they and xe pronouns, "beep." and the song lyrics. *Mitigation:* a per-language localization guide that defines pronoun handling, and an M&E stem plus a textless master for dubbing.

## 14. Production plan: how it gets well produced

* **Picture.** Native 16:9 at **384x216** and **30 fps**, scaled by an integer 5x to 1080p (10x for a 4K master), so pixels stay crisp and the timing ladder is frame-exact. The game's 256-colour palette is the colour script: pastel ramps for HaHaland, LAVA, BONE and FLESH for Hellvile, crayon for history, sepia for flashbacks, and LCD green for the pet. A new camera layer does integer-step pans and parallax. Lip-sync uses the chibi renderer's 8 mouth shapes. Signature transitions are the Wall wipe, hellify and the heart iris.
* **Animatic on day one.** The pilot's beats and durations go into the trailer toolkit's `timeline.c`, which produces a timed animatic with placeholder cards for unwritten scenes. Contact sheets serve as storyboard thumbnails. Music beat times inside each scene are duplicated in `music.c` and must be kept in step by hand until picture and music share named beats.
* **Builds needed.** Chibi presets for Steamy, Mascotty, Kevin, Charlotte, Thumpkin, Tallyworn, Signhere, Clinker and Bolt. A Grumbleguts show model sheet (in uniform) and a GURGLE belly rig. The soot-hand insert and the picture-book prologue.
* **Sound.** A real voice cast:
  * the Mayor, sing-song;
  * Nana, low, slow and warm;
  * Pip, the highest and fastest voice;
  * Grumbleguts, a warm oily baritone;
  * GURGLE, a performer layered with the game's synth babble;
  * Charlotte, a soot-husky giggle;
  * Signhere, rapid patter.

  Add foley over the game's comedy kit (boing, tik, shwip, tink, pluck, creak). Silence is scored as an instrument (20-25% of cutscene time). Every record scratch drops to silence, then a small sting. Mix at 48 kHz/24-bit in stereo and 5.1, with dialogue, music and effects stems. Loudness is **-24 LKFS (ATSC A/85)** for US broadcast, **-23 LUFS (EBU R128)** for Europe, each streamer's own spec for streaming (for example Netflix's -27 LKFS dialogue-gated), and -14 LUFS for social clips. The trailer's -16 LUFS is not a broadcast spec.
* **Music.** "Doomtastic Times" stems drive the main title, the eyecatches and the credits. The underscore uses the game's leitmotifs: HaHaland and Hellvile are one tune in major and minor, there is the Mingle music box, and Grumbleguts gets the 7/8 gate engine. The intensity dial (lullaby, HaHaland, E-minor swing, metal) maps onto act escalation.
* **Deliverables.** Closed captions, including the "[GURGLE: gurgle.]" caption gag. Audio description (essential with a silent lead). Textless versions. An M&E stem. Both the broadcast cut and the streaming cut.
